These are watch estimates, useful for spotting your own trends. They are not medical measurements; anything that worries you is one for a doctor.

{% if data.sleep.dates %}

Sleep

Time in each sleep stage per night, estimated from heart rate, heart rate variability and movement. Deep sleep does most of the physical repair, REM sleep helps memory and mood, and light sleep fills the rest. A few short wake-ups are normal.

  • LowUnder 6 hours, or deep sleep under about 15% of the night, for several nights in a row.
  • Normal7 to 9 hours for most adults. Garmin's targets for you: deep 16 to 33%, REM 21 to 31%, light 30 to 64%.
  • HighMore than 9 hours often. Common after hard training or when ill, otherwise worth noticing.

Sleep score

Garmin's 0 to 100 rating of each night, from how long you slept, the stages, how restless you were and your stress during sleep.

  • PoorBelow 60.
  • Fair to good60 to 79 is fair, 80 to 89 is good.
  • Excellent90 and above.

Sleep against sleep need

Bars are what you slept; the line is how much Garmin thinks you needed that night. Your need goes up after hard training, short nights or a stressful day.

  • Below the lineYou are building sleep debt. One night does not matter; a week of it does.
  • On the lineYou slept about what your body asked for.
  • AboveCatching up, usually after a hard day or a short night.

Heart rate

Daily range, resting and sleeping heart rate

The shaded band runs from your lowest to your highest heart rate each day. The red line is your resting heart rate: the lowest steady rate while you rest, mostly measured at night. The purple line is your average while asleep.

  • Resting, low40 to 60 bpm is common in trained endurance athletes. Lower over months usually means fitter.
  • Resting, typical60 to 100 bpm for adults in general. Compare with your own normal, not with others.
  • Resting, high for you5 bpm or more above your normal for a few days often comes with fatigue, illness, alcohol, heat or poor sleep.

Heart rate variability

{% if data.hrv_last.status %}Status: {{ data.hrv_last.status|title }}{% endif %}

How much the time between heartbeats varies overnight, in milliseconds. More variation usually means a rested, relaxed nervous system. Values differ a lot between people, so only compare with yourself: the green band is your own normal range.

  • Below your bandStress, hard training, illness, alcohol or poor sleep. Take it as a hint to go easier.
  • InsideBalanced. Normal training is fine.
  • AboveOften after rest days. Usually fine on its own.
{% endif %} {% if data.vo2.running %}

VO2max

Running, ml per kg per minute

Garmin's estimate of the most oxygen your body can use, worked out on runs with heart rate and GPS. Garmin shows it rounded; the decimals come from your activity files. It moves slowly, so judge it over months.

  • Going downA drop of 1 or more over a few weeks: a break, illness, or runs in heat and wind, which make the estimate look worse.
  • FlatNormal from week to week.
  • Going upA rise of 1 to 2 over a few months is real progress.
{% endif %} {% if data.daily.dates %}

Pulse ox

Blood oxygen, mostly measured overnight

The share of your blood's haemoglobin carrying oxygen, measured with light at the wrist. Daily average and the lowest reading of the day.

  • LowReadings that stay below 90% are worth raising with a doctor. Single low dips at night are common with wrist sensors (arm position, loose strap).
  • Normal95 to 100% for healthy adults near sea level; low 90s at night is common on a watch.
  • High100% is the maximum.

Breathing rate

Breaths per minute

How many breaths you take per minute: the daily average while awake and the average while asleep.

  • LowUnder about 10 while awake at rest is unusual; during sleep, lower values are normal.
  • NormalAbout 12 to 20 at rest for adults, a bit lower asleep.
  • High for you2 or more above your usual for several days can come with illness, alcohol or heavy training.

Stress

Daily average, 0 to 100

Garmin's stress level from your heart rate variability while you are not exercising. It reacts to mental stress, but also to caffeine, digestion, illness and recovering from training.

  • Rest0 to 25.
  • Low to medium26 to 50 is low, 51 to 75 is medium.
  • High76 to 100.

Stress through the day

Hours in each stress level

How your measured hours split between rest, low, medium and high stress each day. Sleep normally counts as rest.

  • Good dayPlenty of rest hours, with stress in short bursts.
  • MixedMedium and high blocks during work hours are normal.
  • WatchDays with little rest, several in a row.

Body battery

Daily high and low

Garmin's 0 to 100 estimate of your energy reserve. Sleep and rest charge it; activity and stress drain it. The high usually comes after sleep.

  • LowA morning high under 50, or ending days below 25.
  • NormalMorning highs of 60 to 80.
  • HighMorning highs of 80 to 100: well recovered.

Steps

Line: your Garmin step goal

Steps counted by the watch through the day, runs included. Garmin moves your goal up or down based on recent days.

  • LowUnder about 5,000 a day.
  • NormalAbout 7,000 to 10,000 a day is where most of the health benefit shows up in studies.
  • HighAbove 10,000. More is fine, but the extra benefit gets smaller.

Floors climbed

From the barometer

Each floor is about 3 m of climbing on foot, measured by the watch's air pressure sensor (stairs, hills, your estate). Weather changes can add or remove the odd floor.

  • Low0 to 3: a flat day.
  • NormalGarmin's default goal is 10 floors.
  • High20 or more: hilly runs or walks.

Intensity minutes

Per week, moderate plus vigorous

Minutes at a moderate or vigorous effort, counted in blocks of at least 10 minutes. Vigorous minutes count double, as in the World Health Organization guideline.

  • LowUnder 150 a week.
  • Normal150 to 300 a week: the guideline for adults.
  • HighOver 300 a week: typical in training blocks.

Calories

Resting and active, per day

Resting calories are what your body burns to keep running (estimated from weight, height, age and sex). Active calories are movement and exercise, from heart rate and motion.

  • LowMostly resting calories: a quiet day.
  • NormalSeveral hundred active calories on a normal day.
  • HighLong runs and race days can add 1,000 or more.
